A Parent’s Guide: How to Choose the Perfect Acting Class

Aurora Theatre — 24 Apr 2024

Your child is outgoing, loves singing and dancing, and doesn’t shy away from improvising an act in front of a crowd; then why not nurture their talent and enroll them in drama classes?

If you are thinking about whether drama could benefit your child, you are definitely in the right place: we honestly cannot stress enough how important drama is for youngsters.

Drama has the uncanny ability to boost children’s confidence, exposing them to teamwork and critical thinking while providing a supportive environment for growth. However, not every drama class is created equally, and it is important to find the right fit for your child.

Assess Your Child’s Interests and Goals:

Have a conversation with your child about their interests, goals, and motivations for pursuing acting. Consider whether they prefer stage or screen acting, their preferred genres, and any specific skills they want to develop. If they like singing and dancing, they could try musical theatre. Or, if they love stories and characters, drama classes that focus on storytelling and character development might be perfect for them. If your child can’t stand still and movement in in their chords, consider circus and acrobatic classes. Research what’s available to you and address your child towards the right course.

Determine Age-Appropriate Classes:

For kids in drama classes, age matters a lot. Classes should be split by age, with a two to three-year difference between groups. Young kids learn basic acting, storytelling, and creativity, while older ones can tackle more advanced techniques and character development for stage or camera. Check out available courses, review their lesson plans, and ask what they cover during the course.

Research Potential Acting Schools and Instructors:

A great acting coach is everything: they are inspiring, encouraging, and push their students in the right direction. They nurture them and nourish their talent from day one, with care and patience. When researching potential acting schools and instructors, make sure to check their bio and their experience in working with young actors. Read reviews, visit the school’s website, and reach out to current or former students for feedback.

Look for a Supportive and Inclusive Environment:

Choose an acting course that fosters a supportive and inclusive environment where children feel comfortable expressing themselves and taking creative risks. Look for schools and instructors who prioritize collaboration, encouragement, and constructive feedback without judgment or biases.

Attend Trial Classes or Workshops:

Check out what courses around you have free trial classes and take full advantage of them. Very often, schools and institutions generously offer free trial classes to reach out to old and new audiences.  By participating you get to have a feel of the teaching style, class atmosphere, and curriculum. Children can experience first-hand the class of choice, the coaches, and their potential teammates. It’ll be easier to determine if it’s the right fit for them.

Consider Logistics and Scheduling:

Always be realistic when considering logistical factors such as location, class schedule, and costs. Do not over-commit and set unrealistic expectations when going from A to B. It could lead to disappointments or let your child down. It’s a no-brainer, but if a class is far away and you have no transport, you can’t expect other parents to bring your child to class for you (yes: we have seen this behavior several times). Choose a class that is convenient to attend and fits within your child’s schedule without causing unnecessary stress or disruption.

Trust Your Instincts:

As a parent, you know your child better than anyone else. When choosing an acting class for them, trust your instincts and intuition. Don’t be swayed by popular opinion or peer pressure. If a particular class doesn’t feel like the right fit for your child’s interests and goals, keep looking until you find the perfect match. It might require a bit of work from your side, but it will surely pay off in the end.

Drama offers an ample opportunity to develop important life skills while having fun, but remember to take it seriously. Take your time to find the right class that will nurture your child’s passion for acting. To help your child be at ease it’s important to talk to other parents in your community, research courses, and schools, and find a teacher who can help your child’s talent blossom. Once you find the right course, commit to it and make sure to stick to the end to ensure your child receives the full growth and benefits.
