A Parent's Guide: Building Confidence

Aurora Theatre — 8 May 2024

Happy Thursday, everyone- great to be back!

Last week, we discussed the importance of balancing school and acting training in our children’s lives (and if you missed the post you can find it here.

This week, we will be focusing on how to help our young talents build confidence. Developing self-assurance is a crucial skill for young actors on and off the stage or screen. It can take them far from an early age, and it is especially useful in the drama classroom.

In this post, we will explore several techniques and exercises that can help children build a strong sense of confidence. Not only in their acting pursuits but in their personal lives, too.

Introduce your youngsters to improv games

Improvisation games can be a great way for young actors to enhance their confidence and spontaneity. Encourage children to participate in improv exercises.  Remind them to be open about it and not say no (saying yes, and…. is the golden rule of improv).

Children will learn to think on their feet, trust their instincts, and become great listeners.

Need some ideas? Check out this page.

Practice Public Speaking Exercises

Public speaking is a crucial skill for actors, and practicing public speaking exercises can help children feel more confident and comfortable when speaking in front of an audience.

Here’s one for you from us:

To play this game, have a group of children sit in a circle. It’s best to have more than one player, but it’s also possible to play with fewer participants.

One person starts the game by standing up and beginning a story. They have 20 seconds to tell their part of the story. After that, the next person continues the story by adding their element to it. Again, they have 20 seconds to tell their part. This goes on until everyone in the group has had a turn to add to the story.

This game is great for improving listening skills and creativity. It encourages speaking clearly, voice projection, and maintaining eye contact with the other players.

Need more activities? Check this out.

Explore Character Workshops

To effectively portray a character, children must first understand their human side before transitioning into fictional characters.

Introduce your child to self-awareness and self-regulation. Present them to journaling (in case you are wondering about its benefits, read here ) and encourage them to understand their feelings, strengths, and weaknesses. Create a safe space for them to let things out and show them how to manage behavior and emotions. This will help them to develop a positive self-image and self-confidence.

Once you have established a routine for your child, consider enrolling them in character workshops designed for young actors. These workshops offer a chance for young actors to assume different roles and personas, enabling them to explore new perspectives while developing their confidence in acting.

Inspire your child to experiment with different emotions, and embrace the freedom of expression that acting offers.

Remind them to record everything in their journal.

You can also create roleplaying situations where your child can empathize by putting themselves in other people’s shoes.

For example:

“Your schoolmate is sad because their little puppy just died. Find a way to comfort them and make them smile again.”

Scenarios like these help children develop empathy, emotional intelligence, and be good friends.

Incorporate Relaxation Techniques

Relaxation techniques such as deep breathing, visualization, and mindfulness can be of great help to children when it comes to managing nerves and anxiety before auditions, rehearsals, or performances. Encouraging children to practice these techniques regularly can help them develop a sense of calmness and confidence in their acting pursuits. Check out a five-minute guided meditation here.

Mindfulness is a great way for youngsters to learn how to control their nerves, especially onstage

Celebrate Achievements

It’s important to celebrate the achievements of your child, no matter how big or small.

It helps to boost their self-esteem and confidence. It’s crucial to recognize their efforts, applaud their progress, and encourage them to take pride in their accomplishments. Whether it’s mastering a new monologue, landing a role, or delivering a memorable performance, it’s important to acknowledge their hard work and dedication.

Foster a Supportive Environment

Create a supportive and nurturing environment where children feel safe to take risks, make mistakes, and learn from their experiences. Remind them that it’s ok to make mistakes because that’s how we learn.

Inspire positive reinforcement, constructive feedback, and a sense of camaraderie among young actors to foster a culture of confidence and mutual support.

Developing confidence is a journey that requires patience, practice, and perseverance. Young actors can boost their confidence by engaging in improv games, public speaking exercises, character workshops, relaxation techniques, and celebrating their achievements. A supportive environment is also crucial for young actors to build confidence in their acting pursuits and beyond. Allow them to embrace their talents and shine brightly in the spotlight.
