A Parent’s guide: Building Resilience in Young Performers

Aurora Theatre — 23 May 2024

As every actor in the world knows, rejection is an inevitable part of the journey. Young performers are bound to encounter setbacks along the way. It’s not easy to explain to our little ones why they didn’t get the role they wanted. However, learning how to cope with rejection at an early age is essential for building resilience. Maintaining a positive attitude towards adversity creates strong young actors and stronger human beings. This week, we’ll explore strategies and advice for young performers and their parents on navigating rejection with grace and resilience.

Understanding Rejection

Rejection is not a reflection of a young performer’s worth or talent, but rather a natural part of the casting process. Young performers need to understand that casting decisions are often based on a variety of factors, including typecasting, availability, and artistic vision, rather than personal shortcomings.

For more tips on managing rejection, you can check out your previous post here.

Embracing Resilience

Resilience is the ability to bounce back from setbacks and adversity. It’s a skill that can be developed and strengthened over time. Your children can learn to become resilient by viewing rejection as an opportunity for growth and learning. An audition rejection doesn’t have to be a defeat. Self-regulation and self-awareness are particularly useful in this situation, as children can reflect on their areas of improvement and what they would do differently next time. Have open communication with them; remind them that setbacks are temporary. Guide them to own the power to overcome challenges with determination and perseverance.

Maintaining a Positive Attitude

Actors, regardless of age, face countless rejections. This is a normal part of the industry – it’s never personal. Casting directors and producers work tirelessly to find the perfect match for their production. Encourage your child to focus on the aspects of their performance they can control. Emphasize the importance of preparation, effort, and maintaining a positive attitude, rather than fixating on outcomes beyond their control. Help your child understand that not getting a role doesn’t diminish their talent. Remind them to stay positive and approach each audition as an opportunity to showcase their acting skills with enthusiasm and optimism.

Finding Support

The support of family, friends, teachers, and mentors can significantly help young performers deal with rejection. Encourage open communication and provide a safe space for your child to express their feelings and seek guidance when needed. Remind them that setbacks are a natural part of the process and that they are not alone in their journey.

Learning from Rejection

Rejection can be a valuable learning experience that provides insights into areas for improvement and growth. Encourage young performers to reflect on their audition experiences, identify areas for development, and set goals for future auditions. Remind your child that every rejection brings an opportunity to learn, adapt, and return stronger.

Coping with rejection is essential for young actors navigating the competitive performing arts world. By embracing resilience, maintaining a positive attitude, finding support, and learning from rejection, young performers can build the confidence and strength they need to pursue their passion with courage and determination. Remember, it’s not about how many times you fall, but how many times and how you get back up that truly matters.
