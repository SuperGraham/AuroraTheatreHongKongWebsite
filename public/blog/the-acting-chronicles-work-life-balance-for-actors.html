The Acting Chronicles: Work-Life balance for actors

Aurora Theatre — 23 Jan 2024

When we have so much on our plate, it’s really hard to balance everything all around us.

Artists in general, especially actors,  rarely say no to projects and involvement, as they always need to keep an eye out for something that gives them recognition, lets them advance in their careers, and well- pays the bill. However, burnout is a thing, and it’s easy to get overworked and stressed out, and it is also very easy to lose the motivation to go on.

So, what to do to keep everything in place and organised but also how do we allow ourselves to decompress?

Set up a steady morning routine:

Start the day with a consistent morning routine to set a positive tone. Try stretching (like this one here)  and breathing exercises (we like this guided meditation)  to gently wake yourself up.

Make yourself a healthy breakfast to energize both body and mind; unless you are intermittently fasting, skipping breakfast is not a good idea. You’ll get hungry later in the morning, and you’ll be more tempted to grab whatever thing is right in front of you.

Making breakfast at home presents a healthier option, and saves cash too.

Need ideas for a healthy but filling breakfast? Here are some 🙂

Amazing plant-based brekkie options here.

Effective Time Management:

The day of an actor is never the same, especially when juggling multiple things in one go like auditions, freelance work, rehearsals, meetings, and personal life. Irregular schedules can make time management challenging, therefore effective planning is essential. Remember: arriving early is late and arriving earlier is on time. Actors must be seen as reliable, and punctuality is crucial.

If you are struggling to make everything work out, don’t worry: you are not alone.

There are some amazing scheduling apps out there (take a look at these free ones)  that help keep everything in order. Once everything is laid out in front of you it’ll be easier to prioritize tasks and allocate dedicated time blocks.

Self-Care Rituals:

The entertainment industry can be demanding but making room for self-care is crucial for mental and physical well-being. We need to take care of ourselves, prioritize ourselves, and learn when to say no. And if you think saying no doesn’t make you a nice enough person, perhaps you should read this.

Incorporate self-care rituals, such as regular exercise, adequate sleep, nourishing food, and moments of relaxation. Prioritize mental health by engaging in activities that bring joy, whatever they are.

Quality Breaks Between Projects:

Actors often move from one project to another, leaving little time for breaks; yet taking a break and recharging batteries is super important in the life of a creative artist.

When a project finishes, especially a play, our mood is down the drain. We are sad, empty, hollow, and we miss the stage, our cast mates, and the highs that came with the play. It’d be irrational and pointless to embark on another project right away; we wouldn’t have the right set of mind or the right heart for it.

Instead, plan a break that can recharge and refresh you while honouring the work you’ve just finished. You owe it to yourself, and you owe it to your work.

Time/budget is limited, and a proper holiday is out of the question? No problem! How about a micro-adventure?

Setting Boundaries:

It goes without saying, but establishing clear boundaries is vital to prevent burnout. We need to understand what is realistic for us to accomplish, and what isn’t.

Being involved in three productions at a time is counterproductive. We will inevitably arrive tired at each rehearsal and stressed out, not to mention we will frustrate the directors by arriving late or missing rehearsals when the other director expects us to be on the other project without delays and excuses. And truth is you will hate all of that, and you will be counting the days until the production comes to an end. You don’t want this, and no one else does, so be realistic, responsible, and choose. We’ve seen this behaviour far too many times and it gives more harm than good. Not to mention that it is terrible for your stress levels and your mental health.

Communicate limits with agents, directors, and collaborators. Designate specific times for work and personal commitments to maintain a healthy balance. Once again, say no when you need to; don’t be afraid that saying no now will automatically take you off the list for future projects. If you keep yourself visible and available for future opportunities, directors and agents will know where to find you.

Family and Social Connections:

Nurturing personal relationships is essential amidst the demands of the industry. They fulfill us, they enrich us, and they allow us to have the nourishing downtime that we need.

Schedule regular quality time with family and friends. Don’t say you are too busy for them; it might not be possible to schedule something every day, but we owe it to ourselves and to them to spend some quality time together. Actively engage in social activities to maintain a sense of connection outside of work. We are of course passionate about our work, and it is our mission in life, but we can’t just be driven by it. Connect with your loved one even if it’s just for a short time, and let your soul be recharged.

Creative Outlets Outside Acting:

As performing artists, we strive to become better human beings before we become better actors. Diversifying interests contributes to a more well-rounded and fulfilled life, and we shouldn’t give them up because our schedules get too packed. If you don’t know where your time goes, have you considered tracking it down?

We want to know where our money goes, and how many calories we put into our bodies. We should do the same with our time, and be realistic about how much time we spend browsing the internet and social media (check out these suggestions).

Once you have a better idea of where your time goes, allow yourself time to pursue hobbies and creative outlets unrelated to acting, whichever they might be.

This not only offers balance but also enhances personal growth.

Reflecting and Goal Setting:

Reflection and goal setting contribute to a sense of purpose and direction.

Regularly reflecting on achievements and challenges contributes so much to an actor’s personal and professional life.

Always have a journal at hand and use it to set short-term and long-term goals, both within the acting career and personal life. It will be easier to see how your goals evolve and whether changes are needed.

Flexibility and Adaptability:

Yes, this industry is very unpredictable, and it demands flexibility.

Cultivate adaptability by embracing change, even when it is not easy (more on adaptability here) and learning to pivot quickly when needed. Flexibility contributes to resilience.

Digital Detox:

Constant connectivity can be overwhelming, impacting mental well-being, and, let’s face it, exhausting too. And to be fair, we don’t need to be connected 24/7; we can all do without it occasionally.

Introduce regular digital detox periods. If you notice that on Sunday, for example, it’s easier to disconnect, then go for it. You’ll find everything back on when you return to it on Monday.

Foster in-the-moment experiences and mental clarity, and don’t worry about FOMO.

Maintaining work-life balance as an actor requires intentional efforts, but the benefits extend beyond personal well-being. Achieving harmony between work and personal life not only enhances an actor’s overall satisfaction but also contributes to sustained success in the dynamic world of entertainment.

We can see you doing it- you are almost there!

Lots of love,
