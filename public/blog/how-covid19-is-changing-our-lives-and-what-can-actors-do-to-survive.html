How Covid19 is changing our lives (and what can actors do to survive)

Aurora Theatre — 6 Aug 2020

With COVID 19 advancing further and further, actors and performing artists from all over the world are increasingly wondering how the arts scene is going to be affected in the long run and whether acting careers will be able to recover once things do go back to normal. While we can’t answer that question just yet, we can make a vague prediction, and by looking at the world right now it is unlikely that things will go back to normal. We have no clue how long it’ll take for a COVID 19 vaccine to be effective and for how long the virus will linger. Which means we will probably deal with this issue for longer than expected. We might even need to learn how to work around it.

This is not necessarily a bad thing. It’s a change, and change often happens in order to shake us out of our comfort zone. So why not embrace the change and get as proactive as we can?

Here’s what we can do:

Learn self-taping

Auditions are still happening, but no one is willing to travel to and from studios anymore. Learning how to self-tape will open doors to you not only in the location where you are currently at but in other locations, too.

Not sure where to start? This wonderful video will explain the basic tricks, the necessary tools to get started and everything else you need to know.

Think outside the box

Theatre is suffering right now, and it’ll be a while before we get to watch something or perform somewhere. Don’t allow that to bring you down, though. You just need to think creatively: learn how to stream, create content online (Zoom, IG, YouTube- you’ve heard of these, right? ;)), think of outdoors, alternative venues for filming and performing- including your own apartment. This is a real chance to think creatively and explore other opportunities that can still be great for your work- only different.

Keep improving yourself

The news is completely saturated with COVID 19 news, and being overly exposed to it is not necessarily good for your psyche or mental health (we are not saying you should be oblivious to what’s going on around the world, but do you really need to see every single tweet coming from Donald Trump?)

Your brain needs good “food” right now, and positive energy.

Watch movies and TV shows (has anyone watched “I May Destroy You” or “Gangs of London” yet?), use a quiet afternoon to read a book (this resource is free for a month), listen to calming music (we like this), and disconnect from everything and everyone by meditating for a few minutes a day (We like this app, and you can download the iOS or Android version.

Train your memory by memorizing something new every week (poems or monologues, just keep your brain going) and engage with other actors by taking online classes (you can check out what we offer here).

Don’t be shy- gather up and share ideas! Or simply run an improv situation and see where that takes you.

Connect to other actors and artists and collaborate

Our industry is a people’s industry, and a lot can come from two people sharing ideas. We are all in this together: reach out, and connect with fellow artists like you. Something great is just around the corner. You just need to be proactive and make it happen.

Exercise

By keeping your body healthy you have a better chance of fending off viruses and anything nasty that can weaken your immune system. Go out for a walk or light exercise if you can, but if you cannot there’re plenty of resources you can try from the comfort of your living room. For those who are into ballet and barre fitness, we recommend this channel. However, if Zumba or HIIT workouts are better for you, check out these   channels. Do you have a TRX at home? Attach it against the door, and let this be your guide!

Get fit and free as a Hong Kong Ballet’s dancer

Hang in there, and be strong. This is just a lesson sent to us by the universe so that we can learn.

Stay safe and keep creative.
