Don't let Covid 19 and the pandemic take the best out of you.

Aurora Theatre — 23 Jul 2020

Things were starting to pick up – in fact, things were getting better. Life was starting to get back to normal, and activities, social and otherwise, slowly started resuming. You were making plans for the summer, business was picking up, social events were flourishing. And then, boom. A spike in new cases. Another hit of COVID 19 has slammed our community again. No one knows how and why it happened, but we just know it did, and it has taken us back to square one.

In these days and climate, it is hard to keep engaged and motivated, especially when we are actors with no possibility of work coming up, but it is important not to surrender to despair. Actors are like athletes; they must train in order to keep their performance abilities up. Take this opportunity to improve yourself, while you are grateful for the chance to be able to learn something new. COVID 19 can take a serious toll on people’s well-being, and, as we deal with the pandemic, some are more affected than others. Remember: this is just another lesson that has been sent to us so that we can learn. We’ll come out of this stronger than we thought.

– Read:

Whether it is novels or plays, the job of an actor, no matter how experienced or new, is to keep their mind open. It is crucial for actors to use their imagination when exploring a play, or a scene or monologue.  Find your inspiration on printed pages, and put yourself into your character’s shoes. What would you do if you were facing that situation? What could be a possible way out? How would you achieve your goal?

Some interesting (and free) to read     here.

– Learn:

Test your memory and learn a new monologue or a poem. Your memory is like a muscle: the more you train it, the more it gets bigger and stronger.

Focus on your character: who are they, who are they talking to, and why are they saying what they are saying? And also, where are they, when, and what are they wearing? What do they look like?

Use your imagination and your own personal experience. Make choices that are fun for yourself and suitable for your character. Do not put a limit on your imagination. Understanding what your character wants and its background will raise the stakes and push you towards urgency.

Remember to ask yourself what kind of relationship your character has with the person they are speaking to.

A true pursuer of imagination was Stella Adler.

– Practice an accent:

How good is your American, or British, or Aussie dialect? This is the perfect opportunity to get a script out, a recorder, and put some thoughts into it. Need a little inspiration? Find a good one for American    here  (A full course of 5 sessions, completely free. You are welcome).

Need to sound like the Queen of England? Then look    here.

Don’t let another hit of COVID 19 take over your head. If you managed the first time, you can manage this time as well. We have faith in you. Hang in there. Remember we are in this together.
