The Acting Chronicles: Dealing with rejection.

Aurora Theatre — 17 Jan 2024

We’ve all been there.

This role that you are planning to audition for has your name written all over it. It’s perfect for you, and your actor fellows and friends are all telling you that you should totally go for this role.

You are excited. You are prepared. You walk into the audition room with confidence, you make bold choices in your cold read,  you give your full attention to your partner; everyone who watched you engaged in your performance, and you have a great feeling about this.

You are positive that you will get a call back, but days go by and that call doesn’t come. In fact, nothing comes. You get a message a couple of weeks later that says that you didn’t get the job, despite all the efforts and the plus that were on your side.

Why didn’t you get the role?

The simple answer is: who knows?

Perhaps you were a bit too tall for the character, or too short, or too thin, or too chubby, or too muscular, or not muscular enough, or too blonde, or not blonde enough, perhaps your voice was too low, or not deep enough- you get my point.

You’ll never know why you’ve been rejected, yet you must be committed to giving amazing auditions because you never know who is sitting on the panel and who is watching you. You might have not gotten the role, but perhaps someone will remember your stellar audition and recommend you to other people in the industry for other roles. You never know.

Regardless, the competition is fierce; we know that,  and we have known this since the moment we decided to get into acting. We must power through our abilities and move forward to the next thing coming to us. But yes, I get it; rejection sucks, and sadly, it’s unavoidable.

When faced with rejection, it’s important to take time to recoup and self-care, connect to others, share our experience, and listen to others; after all, overcoming rejection is a crucial aspect of an actor’s journey.

Learning from rejection can be a stepping stone to growth, and the tips below not only will allow you to live in harmony through rejection but they’ll help you thrive as an actor and human being.

Take some time to reflect and learn:

After being rejected, it is always worth taking the time for self-reflection; do so in nature if you can, by taking a long walk, journaling, or meditating (and if you are debating whether meditation is good for you, you should read more about it here). It’s now time to unwind and take time for yourself. And if that means you need to binge-watch the latest season of your favorite Netflix series with a tub of ice cream in front of you, well……tomorrow is another day (but please do all of the above as well ? )

Make sure however to analyze the audition or performance objectively, and identify areas for improvement without dwelling on self-blame. Negativity doesn’t do anything for anybody.

You should also consider seeking feedback from trusted mentors or coaches. They know better than anyone else how it is and what it takes to survive in the industry as they’ve been there themselves.

Maintain a Positive Mindset:

Focus on the aspects you can control rather than dwelling on the rejection itself. Cultivate a positive mindset by acknowledging that rejection is a natural part of the industry. Use it as an opportunity to grow and evolve as an actor. There are other projects out there that need your help; it’s just a matter of finding them and being ready for new opportunities- even when they are disguised.

Build Resilience:

Understand that rejection doesn’t define your talent or worth as an actor (and if you want to make yourself feel a bit better now, check this out )

Develop resilience by acknowledging the temporary nature of setbacks; it won’t be like that forever and yes, this too shall pass. Remind yourself of past successes and use rejection as motivation to keep refining your craft. Which areas need work? Perhaps you can enrich your scene portfolio, work on a different accent, or concentrate on writing that play that you had in mind for a long time.

The possibilities are infinite.

Diversify Audition Opportunities:

Expand your audition repertoire by exploring diverse opportunities. Casting directors may have different visions for characters, and widening your range increases your chances of finding the right fit. Remember not to pigeonhole yourself in one specific role: be open, and more will come.

Attend open calls, submit self-tapes, and explore various avenues.

Network and Seek Support:

Build a supportive network within the industry. Meet people, buy coffee, and share stories and experiences with fellow actors who can empathize with rejection. They’ve all been there like yourself. Networking can lead to valuable insights, opportunities, and collaborations that help mitigate the impact of rejection (writing a play about auditioning, anyone? ? )

Focus on Continuous Improvement:

The job of an actor never ends, and you should treat rejection as a stepping stone for improvement. Actors are like athletes: they should always train, no matter whether there is something on the horizon or not.  Channel your energy into honing your skills, taking acting classes, and staying updated on industry trends. The dedication to continuous improvement positions you for future success.

Develop Coping Strategies

Establish healthy coping mechanisms for dealing with rejection, whatever they might be. This might include engaging in activities you enjoy, practicing mindfulness, or seeking emotional support from friends and family. Coping strategies contribute to emotional well-being during challenging times. If you are let down by the surroundings, do not face it alone.

Celebrate Small Wins

Acknowledge and celebrate small achievements along the way. Whether it’s landing a smaller role, receiving positive feedback, or mastering a challenging scene, recognizing these victories reinforces your progress and resilience.

Set Realistic Expectations:

Understand that not every audition will result in success, and that’s normal; but like Phillip Seymour Hoffman said before, auditioning is an opportunity to act in a room where someone else is paying rent (watch his video here). So take advantage of that and make it work for yourself, regardless of what happens afterward. Setting realistic expectations helps manage disappointment. Focus on the process of auditioning and learning, rather than solely on the outcome. Sometimes you are in, sometimes you are out and it’s not just for acting, but for life in general.

Stay Persistent and Patient:

Persistence is key in the entertainment industry. Keep auditioning, learning, and growing. Success often comes through a combination of talent, timing, and perseverance. Be patient with yourself and the journey.

Use these strategies to transform rejection into a constructive force, incorporating it as a catalyst for personal and professional growth in the competitive world of acting.

As we like to say around here: don’t worry, you’ve got this.

Lots of love,

Team Aurora
