The Acting Chronicles: Mastering performance anxiety

Aurora Theatre — 15 Feb 2024

As actors, stepping onto a set or on stage can be exhilarating and nerve-wracking at the same time. And if you think that going on stage in front of an audience is scary, then think about going to auditions. You have less than two minutes to impress the panel with your choices and your way of interpreting the character you are reading, but in truth, they have already made up their mind about you within the first 5 seconds of seeing you.

Pretty scary stuff, huh?

We worry so much about delivering a standout performance that in the end we bury ourselves in performance anxiety- and that is not doing us any favour.

Do not worry though. This week we have put together for you a selection of strategies to help you manage performance anxiety and handle pressure like a pro whether it’s for an audition, a performance, on stage, or set.

Mindfulness Meditation:

When pressure is high, it is paramount that you start your day with calm and focus.

Consider having a mindfulness meditation session to centre yourself and calm your nerves; even if you are in the audition room, find a quiet space/corner, close your eyes, focus on your breath, and use your awareness to be in the moment. Use this guided meditation to cancel the outside noise and focus on yourself.

A simple 5-minute routine will allow you to approach your performance with clarity and confidence and will make you feel great and energized for the rest of your day.

Visualization Techniques:

This is a quick mental exercise that can help you take your performance further. We all know that our brain is an incredibly powerful tool; if we train it with positive thoughts, it will give us the right confidence to face uncomfortable situations. On the other hand, if we fill it up with negativity it will do the opposite of what we want; so best to stick to positive thoughts. Visualize success by imagining yourself delivering a flawless performance. Picture yourself embodying your character with authenticity and conviction, and imagine the positive reactions of the audience or crew members. Have this image repeatedly in your head; it’s a very simple exercise, and yet this mental rehearsal can help alleviate anxiety and boost your confidence.

Breathing Exercises:

As actors, we learn how to breathe at an early stage of our careers; what we need to remember, however, is to use our deep breathing off-stage, too. If you feel your nerves are about to crawl out of your skin, use your diaphragm by inhaling deeply through your nose, allowing your belly to expand, and exhaling slowly through your mouth. Deep breathing exercises will help you regulate your nervous system and calm your mind, and practicing diaphragmatic breathing allows you to reduce stress and elevate relaxation.

Anchoring Techniques:

These techniques are used to create associations in our minds. These techniques are based on psychology and involve linking a specific physical sensation, action, or object to a desired mental or emotional state. In high-pressure situations, it can be helpful to create a physical anchor, such as a touchstone object (like a stone, a pendant, or a piece of jewelry that holds a personal meaning or evokes feelings of confidence or calming)  or a grounding gesture (could be pressing your fingers together or tapping your heart), to help you stay in the present moment. This can help you to feel more grounded and focused and can be a useful tool for managing stress and anxiety.

Positive Affirmations:

You are a gifted and talented human being. Believe it, reaffirm it, and keep repeating positive affirmations to yourself because, once again, if you feed your brain with positive thinking, it will get used to thinking that way and feed you with confidence. Austen Keogh In Steal Like An Artist (available here) gives us a great idea: create a validation file by keeping all the praise you have received about your work, be cards, messages, or emails. No need to look at it every day- you don’t need to constantly boost your ego but keep it for when you need it and enjoy the positive that comes with it. Remember also to think of your talent, training, and past successes, and remind yourself that you are capable of delivering a stellar performance.

Support Network:

Lean on your support network of fellow actors, friends, or mentors for encouragement and reassurance. Reach out to them: share your feelings of anxiety and vulnerability with trusted individuals who can offer support, guidance, and perspective.

Stay Present and Flexible:

Focus on the present moment; that’s the only thing that matters. Anything else can wait.  Listen, engage with your scene partner, and be flexible towards any change of direction they may come your way, or new ideas thrown in by the director. Once you are engaged and in the moment your tension will ease, and you will get to enjoy what is it that you are doing.

Release Perfectionism:

Perfectionism never works. It doesn’t add anything and, if nothing else, it makes you overthink, which is never good. Let go of perfectionism and embrace imperfection as part of the creative process. Make mistakes. If we don’t make them, we are not doing it right. Making mistakes is not only natural but can add depth and authenticity to your performance, and we need them to learn. Let go of the pressure to be flawless and allow yourself to be human.

Professional Preparation:

You have to be prepared. There is no going around it. Practice it until you can’t get it wrong anymore and prepare thoroughly for your performance by familiarizing yourself with your character, script, and scene objectives. Practice everything, from your lines and blocking to emotional beats until they become second nature, giving you a sense of confidence and preparedness on set.

Learn from Experienced Actors:

Talk to experienced actors and be inspired by them; they have navigated performance anxiety and pressure on set and/or stage for a long time and they have a lot of insights to share. Connect with them and their stories and you’ll see that you are not alone in performing and feeling under pressure.

We all have to remember that performance anxiety is a common experience shared by actors of all levels, but once you incorporate some good, solid strategies into your routine and mindset, you can effectively manage anxiety, handle pressure with grace, and deliver performances that leave a lasting impression wherever you may be.

We hope you have enjoyed these five weeks of acting content and we hope these posts have helped you in your acting journey.

Stay tuned and come back again next week, as we might have something else for you ?

Enjoy your weekend!
