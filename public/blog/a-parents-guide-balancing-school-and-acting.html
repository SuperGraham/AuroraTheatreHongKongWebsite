A Parent's Guide: Balancing School and Acting

Aurora Theatre — 3 May 2024

In our last blog post (read it here if you missed it ) we guided you on how to find the perfect acting class for your child.

After enrolling them in the class of their choice, you realize there is quite a bit of juggling around. Balancing school and acting is not for the faint of heart; it can be very challenging. Drama workshops require commitment and they come with tasks to do. It is crucial to maintain a well-organized and disciplined approach to ensure everything runs smoothly.

This week, we will guide parents of young talents on how to plan their child’s acting career and avoid burnout and overwhelming stress.

Time Management:

The earlier we can teach children the importance of effective time management, the better. We can assist children in creating schedules that balance schoolwork, extracurricular activities, and drama programs. Prioritizing tasks and setting achievable goals can prevent dropouts and reduce feelings of being overwhelmed.

Communicate with teachers:

Let your child’s teachers know about their acting commitments. Encourage children to inform their teachers in advance about any upcoming auditions, rehearsals, or performances so that educators can accommodate them if necessary.

Homework Organization:

Teach children strategies for staying organized with their schoolwork. A great way is to use planners (we have created a Monthly planner for you!) so they can keep track of their assignments and deadlines. Remind them to set aside study time and break tasks into manageable chunks. It’s also important to guide them to complete assignments promptly and to seek help if they need to, to avoid falling behind.

Prioritize Health and Well-being:

Your child’s health and well-being is above all else. Make sure your child gets enough sleep, eats nutritious meals (we found amazing ideas and recipes for you here), exercises regularly (if slotting in exercise in your child’s schedule brings more stress, how about a spontaneous long walk in the park?), and take regular breaks to avoid burnout. Remind them that their physical and mental well-being should always come first.

Setting Realistic Expectations:

Help children set realistic expectations about their academic and acting goals. Encourage them to pursue their passion for acting while understanding that it may require sacrifices and hard work. Remind them that success in both school and acting takes time, effort, and dedication.

Seeking Support:

Encourage children to seek support from family, friends, teachers, and mentors when juggling school and acting commitments. Remind them that it’s okay to ask for help when feeling overwhelmed and that they don’t have to navigate this journey alone. You can also introduce them to journaling, which is a great way to nurture self-awareness, mindfulness, and emotional intelligence (some ideas on journaling for you here).

Finding Balance:

Ultimately, it’s all about finding the right balance. Encourage your child to make time for hobbies, socializing with friends, and pursuing other interests outside of acting.

Juggling both acting and school is challenging, but with discipline, passion, and the right tools, your child can excel and benefit in the long term.

Remember to take a breather and guide them through what’s possible, without setting unrealistic expectations or goals.
