The Acting Chronicles: Breaking the mould- Stereotyping and typecasting.

Aurora Theatre — 8 Feb 2024

You landed a role in a film. Congratulations!

Except that the role you’ve been cast in is something that you have played multiple times, and you are about to play it again.

Having something to work on is great but being cast in the same role over and over again doesn’t add much to your portfolio, not to mention that, as an actor, it doesn’t leave a lot of room for growth.

So how can we step away from this happening repeatedly?

First, it is important to understand the difference between typecasting and stereotyping: in films, both situations seem to appear a lot (more than in the theatre, but there are times when both scenarios happen in the thespian world as well).

Typecasting is the act of repeatedly casting actors, in particular roles based on their appearance, background, or previous performances, rather than considering their potential range or talent.

On the other hand, stereotyping involves reducing an actor or character to generalized traits attributed to their ethnic, cultural, or social group.

Yes- it has happened to all of us: it’s annoying, limiting, and, let’s face it, insulting. So what can actors do to minimize the risk of this situation happening?

Diversify Your Portfolio:

Every actor should have a selection of monologues, both classic and contemporary, under their belt. Build a portfolio of solo scenes that can showcase your abilities, and seek out diverse roles across genres, mediums, and character types. Do not shy away from comedy, it’s the genre that is the most popular, and production companies are willing to pay lots of money for it (think of all the cash Matthew Perry has made in his life).

We have seen plenty of actors who only consider super dramatic roles so that they can be taken seriously, but the reality is different. Be brave, and actively pursue roles that are different from the range you currently have been working on. In other words: step out of your comfort zone.

Develop a Range of Skills:

Get yourself in the hands of a fabulous acting coach and keep practicing how to portray a wide range of characters authentically. Have fun with it. Play. See what works and what doesn’t. If something doesn’t work, don’t see it as a waste of time: see it as a learning lesson.

Challenge Casting Norms:

You, actor, have the power to change things (the SAG- AFTRA strike has taught the industry a thing or two), so don’t underestimate this power and use it to your advantage. Advocate for inclusive casting practices: encourage other actors to voice their concerns; create forums and platforms; host talks. Let casting directors and producers hear your voice, and encourage them to consider actors of diverse backgrounds for roles traditionally associated with specific stereotypes.

Create Your Own Content:

Take control of your narrative by creating original content. Write, produce, or collaborate on projects that showcase your unique talents and perspectives, allowing you to break free from typecasting (Lin-Manuel Miranda, anyone? #genius).

Collaborate with Diverse Filmmakers:

Seek out collaborations with filmmakers committed to diversity and representation. We talked about the importance of networking in our previous posts, and it should always be on your list to do. Working with directors and producers who value authenticity can lead to opportunities to transcend stereotypes and offer you a solid opportunity to show different skills.

Embrace Character Depth:

Have a chat with your acting coach and see what suggestions they could have for you.

Their ideas could be something very different from what you have been portraying so far. Regardless of what character you choose, portray them with depth and complexity.

Dig deep into the text and subtext to explore their motivations, flaws, and nuances to create multi-dimensional performances that defy simplistic stereotypes.

Research and Preparation:

No character is on paper only. Every single one of them is written with a precise intention and objective. Every character is human, no matter how well, or badly they are portrayed in the script. Conduct thorough research for each role to understand the character’s background, context, and motivations. Be a text detective and find the most minute nuances that make your characters, and your acting choices, stand out.

Approaching each role with empathy and authenticity will help you to avoid falling into stereotypical portrayals.

Network Strategically:

We said it before, and we are saying it again: in the acting world, networking takes up a much bigger slice than acting itself. Sadly, the entertainment industry has less to do with talent, and more to do with who you know. Not only it is sad, but it is also very true.

Expand your network to include a diverse range of industry professionals. Establish relationships with casting directors, agents, and filmmakers who prioritize casting actors based on talent and suitability for the role, rather than stereotypes. They might be more difficult to find, but we promise you: they exist.

Seek Feedback and Mentorship:

Seek feedback from trusted mentors, coaches, and peers (and when it comes to peers and fellow actors, offer to do the same for them). Surround yourself with (positive) individuals who can provide you with constructive criticism and guidance to help you grow as an actor and avoid being pigeonholed into stereotypes.

Stay True to Yourself:

Ultimately, you need to stay true to your identity and values as an actor- no matter what. Trust your instincts and intuition, and when selecting roles and navigating the industry listen to your gut and your common sense. They rarely fail you.  Your authenticity and individuality are your greatest assets, and they are the ones that will lead you to the path of success.

Don’t be discouraged when you are offered roles that don’t challenge you; open your views and be ready to challenge yourself with something new; with that in mind, you

can proactively challenge typecasting and stereotyping, paving the way for more diverse and authentic representations in the entertainment industry.

That’s all from us at Aurora this week.

Have a great weekend!
