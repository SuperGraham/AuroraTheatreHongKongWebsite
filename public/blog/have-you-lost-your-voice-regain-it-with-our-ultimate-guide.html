Have you lost your voice? Regain it with our ultimate guide!

Aurora Theatre — 25 Oct 2019

This post may contain affiliate links.

The gig is on tonight; you have been already performing for a few days whilst juggling other things at once. It’s tiring (we’ve been there).

Actually, no: it’s exhausting-  and unfortunately, it shows on your body. Perhaps you have strained your voice, or you haven’t correctly warmed up before using it, or maybe you’ve just as well been a bit careless. Maybe those pints/glasses your wine at the pub after the show last night didn’t really help that much.

Bottom line: you are on stage tonight, and your voice is gone. Scary, yes. Agents and talent scouts are going to be in the audience, together with reviewers and, of course, your loved ones.  We know the feeling. We’ve all been there. Not fun.  Yet actors manage to go on stage and deliver -yes, it does sound like a miracle. The mixture of concentration and adrenaline will take you very far, however, your voice still needs a boost.

It’s painful. We know.

Here’s how.

Check out these (tested) suggestions, and try them out.

1) Sssssssssssssssssssssssshhhhhhhhh!

A bit basic perhaps, yet this tip goes a long way: talk little as possible. Or, even better, don’t talk at all. If you are you a teacher by day, very often taking a day off is not always possible. So let’s do with what we have: pair your students and let them work together on a task. Are you a drama teacher? Then let them work on a ten-minute play written by them, and use that lesson to let them concentrate on their work, so that you can rest. You will share their work with feedback on the next lesson.

2) Keep your neck and shoulders warm!

Wrap a scarf around your neck, and make sure you avoid any unexpected chill or air conditioning blasts directly on your neck and shoulders. If you live in Hong Kong and you take buses or MTR to go around town, then you know exactly what we are talking about. If you drive, windows down are a big no-no, no matter how hot it is in your car (keep the ventilation within limits).

We like our pashminas made of cashmere, and here are our favourites!

3) Drink!

We have this miraculous recipe you can make at home and carry with you. But nothing cold, of course.

Take a pot of hot water.

Slice ginger and lemon

Squeeze a generous amount of lemon and throw the rest in the pot.

Add ginger and some good quality honey, like this one (and good value, too).

Put everything to a boil. Turn the gas off and let everything rest for a while.

Make sure to drink it fairly hot.

Sip on this drink all day long. In need of a portable coffee cup/tumbler? Click here– help the environment and save cash on hot drinks.

4) Gargle

We strongly believe (especially Nicole) that tea tree oil cures everything.

You can read more about the properties and the uses of tea tree oil here, but for now, we recommend to add a few drops to a glass of water and gargle every two or three hours during the day. Tea tree oil reduces the inflammation of the throat in no time- just make sure to gargle several times, especially if you are throat is swollen. On a budget? Buy online and save. In a rush, on the go, and no tea tree in the house? Salt will come in handy and will freshen up your vocal cords. Add some to a glass of water and make sure it dissolves (Nicole uses her hometown salt).

5) Pineapple Juice

Did you know? Apparently, it is the number one thing opted by opera singers. It sure worked well among our actors in the past productions. Cut pieces of pineapple and put them in a juicer (invest in a decent one if you don’t have it) . Don’t buy the bottled crap from the supermarket: it’s packed with sugar and it’s nasty. It won’t do you any favour.

6) Inhale Tea Tree Oil

This will help you sleep, and will open up the stuffed area of your nose and throat.

Boil a pot of water, add a few drops of tea tree oil, and put a towel over your head. Keep your eyes closed, as the fumes are quite strong (girls, how about this to cover your eyes?)

Breathe in and out for 15 minutes, and enjoy the feeling that this quick and inexpensive remedy leaves you with.

7) Drink Wasp Tea

Yes. Seriously. As we live in Hong Kong we know all about herbal teas and Chinese medicine. It’s definitely something to try for desperate measure; we did try it and we can say that it works wonders. It tastes God awful though, but if you are in that situation, we are sure you won’t be too squeamish about it. The Chinese name of this tea is 咸竹蜂  (it stands for salty bamboo bee or something of the sort), and you can find it here.

Is your voice back yet? Don’t tell us, we don’t want to know just yet. Tell us after your show. Break a leg always, and stay healthy!
