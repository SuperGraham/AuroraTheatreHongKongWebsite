A Parent's Guide: Audition Success

Aurora Theatre — 16 May 2024

Auditions can be thrilling and nerve-wracking experiences for young performers and their parents. And trust us, not only for young performers: everyone who auditions or has auditioned in the past can agree that it’s not a relaxing experience.

This week we’ll share practical tips and guidance to help children prepare for auditions, manage nerves, and showcase their talents with confidence (and if you missed the memo about confidence building in children here it is again).

We will also offer advice for parents on supporting their child through the audition process and what to expect from the often unpredictable world of auditions. So stay on this page!

Be prepared!

Preparing for auditions involves more than memorizing lines; it requires careful preparation and practice to showcase your abilities effectively.

First, research the role and the production and understand the character they’re auditioning for. Research the director, the production style, and any specific requirements or expectations.  Check their previous work and who they worked with.

See whether the role they are looking for fits you, and select and practice appropriate audition material, whether it’s a monologue or a song.

Memorizing Lines and Practicing Monologues

Monologues, or solo scenes, are essential components of audition preparation.

No matter how young or old, every talent should have at least one monologue prepared. The material you choose should meet the tone of the production you are auditioning for. If you are auditioning for a comedic role, you should have at least one comedic monologue under your belt.

Learning lines, however, is not the biggest obstacle for actors. Understanding a scene, its subtext, and its characters will allow you to make choices that elevate your character from the page to the stage. Remember to break down the script into smaller, manageable chunks, and practice regularly.

Ask yourself the following questions:

Who are you?

Where are you?

What do you want?

What gets in your way?

How are you going to get what you want?

Ask yourself “What is the scene about?”

And then, after some careful thinking,  ask yourself “What is it really about?”

What conflict/difficulties is the character facing? What are they struggling with?

The more you understand what the character wants and their intentions, the more you’ll be able to make bold, creative choices to showcase your acting skills (here is a great monologue resource for children).

Staying Calm Under Pressure

Auditions can be nerve-wracking, but staying calm under pressure is crucial for success. Learn how to breathe and refocus with a nice, guided meditation (we like this one).

Remind your children to focus on their preparation and trust in their abilities, rather than letting fear or self-doubt hold them back.

Warm-Up Before Auditions

Just as athletes warm up before a game, you should encourage your children to warm up their bodies and voices before auditions. This can help them feel more relaxed and prepared to perform at their best. Simple warm-up exercises like stretching, vocalizing, and breathing can greatly elevate confidence and readiness.

Not sure what warm-ups to do? Here’s something for you!

Dress Appropriately

Guide children on dressing appropriately for auditions, considering the character they’re auditioning for, and the production’s style. Guide them through choosing comfortable, professional outfits that reflect the character’s personality or the production’s tone. Avoid overly distracting or elaborate costumes that may detract from their performance.

Arrive Early and Be Prepared

To arrive early is to arrive on time.

Arriving early for auditions allows time to sign in, fill out the necessary paperwork, and mentally prepare. Have all required materials ready such as headshots, resumes, and audition materials in an organized manner.

Being prepared and punctual demonstrates professionalism and respect for the audition process. Not sure what to include in your child’s portfolio? Read here!

Be Open to Direction

Remind children to be open to direction and feedback from casting directors or audition panelists during auditions. Encourage them to listen attentively, take direction positively, and be willing to make adjustments to their performance if requested. Demonstrating flexibility and adaptability can leave a positive impression on casting professionals, regardless of whether you’ll be offered a role or not.

One of the best advice ever received comes from New York acting coach Maggie Flanigan (more about her amazing studio here): always worry about giving a great audition, even though they cannot cast you this time. They will remember you for that, and maybe offer you a different role another time, or pass your contact on to other casting directors.

Maintain Professionalism Throughout

Your child should maintain professionalism and courtesy throughout the audition process, from the moment they enter the audition room to the moment they leave. Remind them to greet casting professionals with a smile, introduce themselves confidently, and thank them for the opportunity to audition. Remind them to stay focused, respectful, and engaged throughout the audition experience.

Learn from Each Audition Experience

Each audition is a learning opportunity, regardless of the outcome. Remind them to self-reflect on their audition experience, identify areas for improvement, and apply what they’ve learned to future auditions. Every audition, whether successful or not, brings valuable insights and growth opportunities for young performers.

Navigating the Audition Process

For parents, navigating the audition process can be overwhelming. Guide your child on practical matters such as researching audition requirements, scheduling auditions, and ensuring they have appropriate attire and materials. Just a reminder: don’t take on all these tasks alone. Involve your child so they can learn about responsibilities and self-management. Provide emotional support and encouragement, and help set realistic expectations by emphasizing the value of the audition experience, regardless of the outcome.

Managing Expectations

Managing expectations is crucial for both children and parents during the audition process. Youngsters need to understand that auditions are opportunities to showcase their talents and learn and grow as performers, rather than tests of their worth or abilities.

As Philip Seymour Hoffman said: “Auditioning is a chance to act in a room someone else is paying rent for.”

An audition is a chance to practice acting, and to learn something new. If, for whatever reason, you do not get cast in the role, reflect on what you would have done differently and what you have learned, rather than dwelling on what you didn’t get this time.

We know: auditions can be challenging.

However, with careful preparation, practice, and support, young performers can confidently navigate the process. By offering practical tips for audition preparation, learning relaxation techniques to manage nerves, and providing guidance and emotional support, we can empower young performers to showcase their talents and pursue their dreams with confidence and resilience.

It is important for us that young performers and their parents have an understanding of the audition process and feel better equipped to navigate auditions successfully.

Share with us your experience and your audition stories: we’d love to hear from you!

Have a great weekend!
