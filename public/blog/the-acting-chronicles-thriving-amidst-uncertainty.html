The Acting Chronicles: Thriving amidst uncertainty.

Aurora Theatre — 30 Jan 2024

It’s no secret that the acting industry changes constantly, and that instability in acting is everywhere; that’s the reason why it is crucial for actors not only to know how to navigate industry uncertainty but to thrive in it as well.

Focusing on growth, not only as an actor but as a human being, is the best thing actors can do. If we are prepared to grow and improve while the world evolves and changes around us, we will have the power and the strength to face those changes and allow them to become part of our lives.

This week we are bringing you ten ideas elaborating on strategies to handle the unpredictable nature of the entertainment industry. Adapting to showbiz requires mental strength and adaptability, but it doesn’t have to be mentally and physically draining.

Continuous Learning and Skill Development:

Embrace a mindset of lifelong learning; everyone, not only actors and artists, should challenge themselves to learn new things and step out of their comfort zone. Consider engaging regularly in acting classes, workshops, and skill development to stay relevant in an ever-evolving industry. Polish up your acting skills; add new scenes to your portfolio; master an accent, or even more than one if you can. Remember: actors are like athletes. If they don’t train every day, they will lose it.

Be adventurous and go join that improv group that you have heard about, or explore different acting techniques that perhaps you tried once but didn’t have the chance to explore further. Or how about reading those books that have been sitting on your shell for a very long time?

Be brave, be adventurous, be curious. (Need something to read? Here are a few suggestions)

2.  Networking and Building Relationships:

In the acting and entertainment industry, networking is everything, and at times who we know matters more than what we know.

Cultivating a robust professional network is crucial for our acting career; building relationships with industry peers, casting directors, and producers can open doors to opportunities and provide valuable insights during uncertain times.

Have your business cards ready (you can make them yourself easily on Canva), activate your charming self, and go talk to people. Ask relevant questions, and be engaged and prepared when going to a talk or workshop of a particular casting director or producer. Check out their work and who they have previously worked with.

You can sign up to IMDB Pro  and look anyone up (the premium version offers a lot of perks, and you can have a 30-day trial).

Lots of events are happening online, and out there are plenty of opportunities to expand your geographical location. The Actor’s Fast Track offers lots of online events with producers and casting directors for a very reasonable price, together with branding coaching for actors.

Diversification of Skills:

Exploring roles beyond acting will give you a wider range of job opportunities.

If everything makes sense to you only when it’s organized and in place, consider producing or stage managing. If you have a knack for writing, invite your friends to read your play over snacks and make your project happen.  Do you have friends who produce incredible writing? Offer to direct their pieces or help in a short film in any capacity you can. It doesn’t have to be always about acting and keeping ourselves open-minded and willing to get involved in different things will increase versatility and opening up opportunities in various facets of the industry.

Financial Planning and Budgeting:

Let’s face it: this industry is expensive. Actors need a lot of cash flow at their disposal for classes, workshops, headshots, memberships, etc, not to mention the lifestyle and life basics that need to be covered (rent, food, bills, transportation, family, and so forth).

When the industry is so unpredictable, establishing a financial safety net can give a bit of room to breathe when the times get tough. Uncertainty can no doubt impact income stability, so having a well-thought-out budget and savings plan provides a buffer during lean periods. First, having a clear idea of how much money comes in and goes out each month will put everything into perspective, and help you save or ditch those expenses that don’t need to be there in the first place. Use apps to track your finances (we like Back in Black) and work towards gaining financial freedom.

Staying Adaptable to Change:

The world is in constant evolution. Think about it: how much has the world changed since the day you were born? How did we adapt to those changes? Was it difficult? Was it painful? Did you struggle?

Sometimes change is good, other times not so much, but we must embrace change as a constant. Adaptability is our ally. The industry evolves rapidly, and actors who adapt quickly to new trends, technologies, and industry shifts are better positioned for long-term success.

6.  Being Proactive in Self-Promotion:

If networking is crucial for actors, self-promotion is just as crucial and goes hand in hand with it. Leverage social media, create an online presence, and actively showcase your work to increase visibility, especially during times of uncertainty. Furthermore, creating a community helps you to stand out from the crowd. Create opportunities for people to collaborate, share your areas of expertise, and inspire people. Not only it will help you to connect with artists and various people in the industry, but it will make you connect at a deeper level with your community while taking control of your own brand.

Exploring Independent Projects:

Consider independent projects, whatever they may be. Be open to opportunities even if payment is not always on the table, whether it’s students’ films or community theatre. It’s an opportunity to act, and perhaps to have free footage for your new showreel, or connect and meet new people.

Participating in or initiating independent productions allows actors to take creative control and create opportunities outside the traditional industry structure.

Flexibility in Project Selection:

Everyone has a go-to role, the one we feel safe and secure in and the one that helps to guarantee us jobs, but as actors we should always challenge ourselves and be open to diverse roles. After all, the industry’s landscape is dynamic, and if we want to fit in, we must be willing to take on a variety of roles that can lead to unexpected and fulfilling opportunities.

Experiment and have fun with roles you haven’t quite thought about and see where that leads you and what opens up for you.

Seeking Professional Guidance:

Actors must be connected and have a strong grasp of reality. That’s why consulting industry professionals like agents, career coaches, or mentors is so important, as they are highly skilled professionals who can provide valuable insights, guidance, and a strategic perspective on navigating uncertainties in the entertainment business.

Roger Simon from the RSS  in New York gives great guidance and acting coaching to actors (both face-to-face and online), whereas Valorie Hubbard from The Actor’s Fast Track provides business coaching and branding for actors, sharing loads of tips and insights from the industry.

Mental Resilience and Positive Mindset:

We have already mentioned in our previous posts how important it is to develop a positive mindset; focusing on the bright side of things helps actors weather uncertainties with grace. Focus on the aspects within your control and view challenges as opportunities for growth, without letting circumstances slamming you down.

Remember: “Nothing is permanent in this wicked world, not even our troubles (Charlie Chaplin).

It’s not always easy to face challenges and thrive through change, but with a positive mindset, an optimistic vision, and open-mindedness, actors can proactively address industry uncertainty and position themselves to shine in a field known for its ever-changing nature. Are you going through changes at this particular time of your life? What works, and what doesn’t for you?

Send us a note, or leave a comment, and share your thoughts with us.

See you next week!
